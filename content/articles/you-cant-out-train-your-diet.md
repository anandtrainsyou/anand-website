An hour of steady running burns somewhere around 600 calories for most people. A big plate of biryani with a cold drink can be more than that, and it takes ten minutes to eat.

That's not a reason to skip the gym. It's a reason to stop expecting the gym to do a job it was never built for.

## Training builds the body. Food decides the scale.

Fat loss comes down to one thing: eating a little less energy than you burn, consistently, for long enough. Training matters a lot, but mostly for *what* you lose. Lift while you're in a deficit and more of what comes off is fat, not muscle. Skip it and you end up smaller but softer.

So think of it like this:

- **Nutrition** controls whether you lose weight.
- **Training** controls what that weight is made of.
- **Sleep and steps** make both of those easier.

> If you're training hard and the scale isn't moving, the answer is almost never "train harder". It's "look at what you're eating".

## Why this is good news

If the diet is the lever, you don't need to live in the gym. Three or four solid sessions a week is plenty. The rest happens in the kitchen, in small, boring, repeatable decisions:

1. Get protein in every meal.
2. Keep a rough eye on portions of the calorie-dense stuff: oil, ghee, rice, sweets, drinks.
3. Walk more than you think you need to.

You don't need to cut out carbs, stop eating after 7pm or drink anything green. You need a plan you can follow on a bad week, not just a good one.

## The takeaway

Train to build the body you want. Eat to reveal it. Don't try to run off a diet you haven't planned.
