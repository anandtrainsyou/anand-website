"I couldn't walk for three days. Great leg day."

I get the appeal. Soreness feels like proof. But it's a bad way to judge a workout, and chasing it can actually slow you down.

## What soreness really tells you

Muscle soreness mostly shows up when you do something *new* or *different*: a new exercise, more volume than usual, or a lot of slow lowering. Do the same thing again a week later and you'll be far less sore, even though the workout was just as effective.

So soreness tells you something was unfamiliar. It doesn't tell you it worked.

## Why chasing it backfires

If you program for soreness, you end up:

- changing exercises constantly, so you never get good at any of them
- piling on junk volume to "feel it"
- turning up to the next session too beat up to train well

That's the opposite of what builds muscle and strength, which is **doing a bit more over time on the same lifts**.

> Track the stat that actually levels you up: reps and weight on the bar, written down, week over week.

## A better scoreboard

Keep a simple log. If your lifts are slowly going up and your recovery is decent, the program is working, sore or not. If they've stalled for a few weeks, *then* change something.

Feeling a bit sore is fine. Just don't let it be the reason you train.
