There's a lot of noise around fat loss. Most of it is about the details that matter least. Here's what actually moves the needle, roughly in order of importance.

## 1. A calorie deficit

You lose fat when you consistently take in a little less energy than you burn. Every diet that works (keto, intermittent fasting, "clean eating") works by getting you there in a different way.

**Start here:** aim to lose around 0.5–1% of your bodyweight per week. Faster than that and you'll usually lose more muscle and feel terrible.

## 2. Enough protein

Protein keeps you full and protects muscle while you diet. A good target for most people is roughly **1.6–2.2 g per kg of bodyweight** per day, spread over your meals.

- Eggs, paneer, curd/Greek yoghurt, chicken, fish, dal + soy, whey: all count.
- Build each meal around a protein source first, then add the rest.

## 3. Lifting weights

Lifting tells your body to keep its muscle while you're eating less. Two to four full-body or upper/lower sessions a week is plenty. Focus on getting a little stronger over time.

> Not sure where to start? The [Full Body Foundations plan](/guides/?g=full-body-3) is free.

## 4. Daily movement

Steps quietly burn more than most workouts. Walking is easy to recover from and easy to stick with. Find your current daily average and try to add 2,000 on top.

## 5. Sleep

Short sleep makes you hungrier, weaker in the gym, and worse at sticking to anything. Seven hours or more is the goal. It's the cheapest performance boost there is.

## 6. Consistency over perfection

A plan you follow 80% of the time for six months beats a perfect plan you quit after three weeks. Have a "bad day" plan: hit your protein, go for a walk, move on.

---

## What you can ignore (for now)

- Meal timing and "no eating after 7pm"
- Detoxes, cleanses and fat-burner supplements
- Cutting out entire food groups
- Fasted cardio vs. fed cardio

Nail the six things above for a few months before worrying about any of these.
